# Korean tax notes (checked 2026-11-25)

Simplified summary used by `src/insiderlab/tax.py`. Not tax advice; re-check at every
quarterly review.

| Holding | How gains are taxed | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| US-listed shares (NMM, IBM, NVDA, MRVL, any signal stock) | Gains and losses netted over the calendar year; 22% (20% + 2% local) on the net gain above a 2.5M KRW basic deduction; filed the following May | [wikidocs, 2026](https://wikidocs.net/blog/@TaxSave/26706/) |
| TIGER S&P 500 (Korea-listed overseas-index ETF), ordinary account | Trading gains and distributions taxed 15.4% as dividend income, no deduction; counts towards the 20M KRW financial-income threshold | [KB Asset Management](https://kbam.co.kr/insights/guide/etf-tax-guide-domestic-vs-other-types) |
| Same ETF in an ISA | Losses net inside the account; 2M KRW tax-free (general type), 9.9% above that | [Asia Economy, 2026-08-19](https://core.asiae.co.kr/article/2026081915244676925) |
| 2026 RIA (return-to-domestic-market account) relief | Only for overseas shares held on 2025-12-23, sold and reinvested in Korean stocks | [Dailian](https://www.dailian.co.kr/news/view/1593022) |

What this means at about 1.5M KRW: US-stock gains are very unlikely to reach the 2.5M KRW
deduction, so the US leg is effectively untaxed, while the ETF in an ordinary account pays
15.4% on any gain. Whether TIGER sits in an ordinary account, an ISA or a pension account
changes the after-tax benchmark (memo decision 7; the default assumption is an ordinary
account).
